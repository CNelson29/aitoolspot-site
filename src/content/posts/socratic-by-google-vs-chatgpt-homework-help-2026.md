## What is Socratic by Google?

Socratic by Google is a free educational app acquired by Google in 2018 and woven into the Google Lens ecosystem. Students photograph a homework problem — printed text, handwritten equations, or diagrams — and receive step-by-step visual explanations, curated videos, and subject breakdowns at no cost. In 2026 it remains one of the only truly free AI homework helpers on the market, with no paid tier and no upsell.

The matchup students search most is **Socratic by Google vs ChatGPT for homework help**. The core difference: Socratic was built from the ground up for students; ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant that students repurpose. That shapes everything — interface design, pedagogical approach, pricing, and subject depth.

## Key Features

- **Photo-based question scanning** — Point your camera at a printed or handwritten problem; Socratic identifies the question and returns explanations without typing.
- **Step-by-step visual breakdowns** — Answers come with diagrams, annotated steps, and embedded educational videos, prioritizing understanding over speed.
- **Multi-subject coverage** — Math, science, history, and literature at the high-school level.
- **Google Lens integration** — Google's image-recognition stack improves OCR accuracy for equations and diagrams.
- **Mobile-first design** — Available on iOS and Android; built for on-the-go homework sessions.

## Pricing

**Socratic by Google is completely free.** There are no paid plans, no premium tier, and no subscription required. Google funds the product as part of its educational suite.

ChatGPT offers a free tier with usage caps, a Plus plan at $20 per month, and a Pro plan at $200 per month. For most homework tasks the free tier covers typical student needs, though Plus unlocks faster responses and higher usage limits.

## Pros and Cons

### Socratic by Google — Pros

- Completely free with no hidden costs or upsell
- Photo input is fast and accurate for printed and handwritten problems
- Curated educational content — videos and diagrams — instead of raw text dumps
- Designed to teach concepts, not just hand over answers
- No ads or subscription pressure

### Socratic by Google — Cons

- Limited to high-school-level content; struggles with college coursework
- Mobile only — no web version
- No conversational follow-up; each question is handled in isolation
- Weak for open-ended writing or essay help

### ChatGPT for Homework — Pros

- Handles any subject at any level
- Strong multi-turn conversation — ask follow-up questions naturally
- Excellent for essays, creative writing, and code

### ChatGPT for Homework — Cons

- Not pedagogically designed — delivers direct answers unless you explicitly prompt it otherwise
- Photo-based math input exists but is not optimized for mathematical notation
- Free tier has usage limits; advanced features require $20 per month

## Who Should NOT Use Socratic by Google

- **College or graduate students** — Socratic is tuned to high-school curriculum; advanced coursework will hit its ceiling quickly.
- **Students who need writing or essay help** — Socratic excels at STEM and factual subjects, not long-form composition or literary analysis.
- **Desktop-first users** — There is no web version. If you work primarily on a laptop, ChatGPT or Khanmigo are better fits.
- **Anyone wanting conversational tutoring** — Socratic handles one question at a time with no memory of previous exchanges.

## Verdict

For a middle or high school student who wants to understand a specific problem — not just copy an answer — **Socratic by Google is the stronger default choice in 2026**. It is free, fast, and built around explaining concepts rather than handing over solutions. The photo-scanning feature alone removes the friction that keeps students from checking their own work.

ChatGPT wins on versatility: it handles college-level material, extended writing tasks, code, and multi-turn tutoring sessions. For students willing to prompt it carefully — asking it to explain step by step rather than just give an answer — it becomes a capable homework companion. Left to default behavior, however, it delivers direct answers rather than guiding students through reasoning, which is the opposite of what most educators want.

Practical split: use Socratic for STEM problem sets in grades 6–12; reach for ChatGPT for writing, research, and anything beyond high-school scope.

## FAQ

### Is Socratic by Google still free in 2026?

Yes. Socratic by Google is completely free with no paid plans or subscription required.

### Can Socratic replace ChatGPT for homework?

For high-school STEM problems, Socratic is often the better tool — it explains concepts step by step rather than delivering raw answers. For college-level work, writing, or coding, ChatGPT is the stronger choice.

### Does Socratic work for math?

Yes. Math is one of Socratic's strongest subjects. Photograph an equation and receive a step-by-step visual solution with annotated workings.

### What is the best free homework AI in 2026?

For high-school students, Socratic by Google is a top pick: completely free and purpose-built for photo-based problem scanning. ChatGPT's free tier and Khanmigo are also strong options depending on subject and level.
