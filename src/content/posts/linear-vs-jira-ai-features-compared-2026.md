## What is Linear?

Linear is a project management tool built specifically for software teams that value speed over configurability. Unlike Jira, which evolved from a bug tracker into a sprawling enterprise platform, Linear was designed from scratch with a keyboard-first, opinionated workflow. In 2026, both tools have added AI capabilities — but they have taken very different approaches, and the gap between them matters for engineering teams choosing a stack.

## Key Features

### Linear AI Features

- **Auto-triage:** Linear's AI automatically categorizes and prioritizes incoming issues, reducing manual backlog grooming for engineering leads.
- **Spec drafting:** AI can draft issue descriptions and technical specs from rough notes — a practical time-saver on fast-moving teams.
- **Git and code context:** Linear's AI connects with GitHub and Claude Code to surface relevant code context directly inside issues.
- **Cycle planning suggestions:** AI recommends which issues should enter the current sprint based on team velocity and priority signals.

### Jira AI Features (Atlassian Intelligence)

- **Smart summaries:** Summarize long issue threads and comment chains.
- **Natural language search:** Query your backlog in plain English.
- **AI-generated descriptions:** Write issue content from a few keywords.
- **Smart workflow suggestions:** Suggest automation rules based on observed team behavior.

According to multiple 2026 comparisons, Jira's AI feels added on rather than native. For engineering teams, Linear's AI offers more actionable workflow automation, while Jira's AI still centers on summarization and search.

## Pricing

Linear and Jira are close on price, but the free tier difference is significant for growing teams.

| Plan | Linear | Jira Cloud |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Unlimited members, 250 issues | Up to 10 users, 2 GB storage |
| Basic / Standard | ~$8–$10/user/month | $8.15/user/month |
| Business / Premium | ~$14–$16/user/month | $16/user/month |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom |

The critical difference is the free tier. Jira caps at 10 users — the moment you hire an 11th person, you pay for all 11 seats immediately. Linear lets your entire company join before hitting the 250-issue ceiling. For teams beyond 30 people, Linear's lower admin overhead also reduces total cost of ownership.

## Pros and Cons

### Linear Pros

- AI built into the core workflow, not a separate feature layer
- Far more generous free tier for growing teams
- Fast, keyboard-first UX with minimal configuration required
- Strong GitHub and Claude Code integration
- Lower admin overhead at scale compared to Jira

### Linear Cons

- Less configurable than Jira for complex enterprise workflows
- Smaller integration ecosystem overall
- Opinionated structure is not ideal for non-engineering teams
- 250-issue cap on free tier can be limiting for active teams

## Who Should NOT Use Linear

- **Atlassian-dependent enterprises:** If your stack already runs on Confluence, Bitbucket, and Atlassian products, Jira's integration depth is very difficult to replace.
- **Non-engineering teams:** Linear is opinionated about software development workflows. Marketing, HR, and legal teams will find Jira or other tools more flexible.
- **Teams needing deep compliance and audit trails:** Enterprise-grade permission structures and audit logging are more mature in Jira.
- **Large orgs already standardized on Atlassian:** Migration costs at 500-plus seats will likely outweigh Linear's UX and AI benefits.

## Verdict

In 2026, **Linear wins the AI features comparison for engineering-focused teams**. Its AI capabilities — particularly auto-triage and spec drafting — are deeply integrated into the core workflow rather than layered on top. Jira's Atlassian Intelligence covers the basics but consistently reads as a reactive addition to an older architecture.

That said, Jira is not standing still, and for enterprises already invested in the Atlassian ecosystem, the switching cost is real. The choice in 2026 comes down to your existing stack more than AI features alone: startups and product-led engineering teams will gain speed and simplicity with Linear; large enterprises on Atlassian are better served staying put and pushing Jira's AI roadmap forward.

## FAQ

### Is Linear free in 2026?

Yes. Linear's free tier supports unlimited team members with up to 250 issues — significantly more generous than Jira's hard cap of 10 users on its free plan.

### Does Linear have better AI features than Jira?

For engineering teams, yes. Linear's AI — including auto-triage and spec drafting — is integrated into the core product. Jira's Atlassian Intelligence handles summarization and search well but is considered less developer-focused by most 2026 reviewers.

### How does Linear pricing compare to Jira in 2026?

Paid plans are broadly similar: both tools start around $8/user/month at the entry tier, rising to roughly $14–$16/user/month at the mid tier. The meaningful difference is Linear's unlimited-member free tier versus Jira's 10-user cap. Always check each vendor's current pricing page before committing.

### Should I switch from Jira to Linear?

If you run a startup or engineering-focused team and are not tied to the Atlassian ecosystem, Linear's speed, UX, and AI integration make it worth a serious evaluation. If you are deep in Atlassian tooling or have complex enterprise compliance needs, the pragmatic choice is to stay on Jira.

### Which tool is better for AI-assisted project management in 2026?

Linear leads for product-led engineering teams; Jira leads for enterprises already running on Atlassian. The decision is driven more by your existing stack than by AI features alone, but Linear's AI depth is a genuine differentiator for developer-centric organizations.
